About this book

This book began as a research document on the authority of Jesus Christ over demons and unclean spirits. It has been developed into a teaching book for practical use in deliverance ministry.

It is written for:

  • pastors and church leaders,
  • deliverance and prayer-ministry teams,
  • Bible school and discipleship teachers,
  • Christians who want a biblical, Christ-centred understanding of spiritual authority,
  • anyone seeking a safe, sober, and scripturally grounded approach to deliverance praxis.

The book is intentionally Christ-centred before it is demon-centred. It studies the enemy only enough to understand the victory of the King. It refuses sensationalism, fear-driven speculation, and the importing of later demonological systems into the biblical text.

The governing conviction is simple:

Jesus Christ is not merely more powerful than demons. He is Lord over the entire spiritual order.

How to use this book

The book has six parts.

  1. Part I — The Foundations of Divine Authority establishes the biblical and lexical foundations.
  2. Part II — The Demonstrations of Christ’s Authority walks through the Gospels, the Kingdom conflict, the Cross, the Resurrection, and the Name of Jesus.
  3. Part III — The Limits of the Enemy and the Believer’s Stand sets out what the enemy cannot do and how believers stand firm.
  4. Part IV — Practical Deliverance Praxis provides a sober, safe, and flexible ministry framework: preparation, discernment, session protocol, aftercare, ethics, and safeguarding.
  5. Part V — A Ten-Lesson Teaching Curriculum turns the material into a teachable course.
  6. Part VI — Appendices contains the Scripture library, study questions, glossary, forms, checklists, aftercare plan, research verification notes, and further resources.

You can read it straight through, or use it as a curriculum. Each chapter ends with Teaching Notes or Praxis Notes to help leaders apply the material.

A word on sources and translations

Scripture quotations are from the Authorized (King James) Version unless otherwise noted. The original-language discussion uses standard lexical data:

  • Greek: Strong’s numbers, Thayer’s lexicon, Abbott-Smith, Moulton-Milligan, and standard critical interlinears.
  • Hebrew: Brown-Driver-Briggs, Strong’s, and standard Hebrew lexicons.
  • Where a claim is a theological inference rather than a lexical fact, the book says so.

A fuller verification report is provided separately in Research_Verification_Report.md. The original research file DivAuth and the summary diva are preserved untouched, as are the companion studies 7SonsSceva and casestudy. Backups exist as DivAuth.backup and diva.backup.

Safeguarding and scope

This book is a theological and pastoral resource. It is not a substitute for medical care, psychiatric care, counselling, or emergency services.

Before any practical use, read Part IV carefully. In summary:

  • Do not physically restrain, hit, choke, or harm anyone.
  • Do not withhold medication, food, sleep, or medical care.
  • Obtain informed consent. A person may stop at any time.
  • Use a two-adult rule. Never minister alone to a minor or vulnerable adult.
  • Refer for psychosis, delusions, hallucinations, self-harm, suicide risk, medical symptoms, abuse disclosure, or substance withdrawal.
  • Do not charge for deliverance.
  • Do not diagnose mental illness as demonisation.
  • Do not use recovered-memory techniques or coercive accusation.
  • Keep confidentiality, with clear limits for safety and legal duties.

These safeguards are not a lack of faith. They are part of loving your neighbour.
