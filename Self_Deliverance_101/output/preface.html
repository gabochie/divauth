About this book

This is the first release of Self-Deliverance 101. It covers Parts One to Four — the theological foundation and the practical methodology — in twenty-seven chapters, together with the F.R.E.E.D.O.M. framework, the tools, and the beginning of the 30-Day Freedom and Renewal Programme.

The central claim is simple:

You do not have to remain in bondage to what Jesus Christ has already defeated.

This book is not about becoming obsessed with demons. It is about learning to live in the freedom Christ secured, and then growing into the person God made you to be. Deliverance is the beginning of a transformed life, not the final destination.

The book is deliberately Christ-centred. The enemy is not the hero of this story, and the method is not the saviour. Christ is the Deliverer. The believer responds to His truth in faith, repentance, obedience, resistance, and discipleship.

How to use this book

Read the four parts in order. Parts One to Three establish what you believe; Part Four shows you how to live it. Do not skip the foundations to get to the exercises — the exercises are only safe when they rest on truth.

Each chapter closes with three things:

  • Key Scriptures — the texts to read and meditate on.
  • Exercise — a practical response, never a test you can fail.
  • Teaching Notes — the points to retain or to discuss in a group.

The F.R.E.E.D.O.M. framework introduced in Part Four is an organisational framework for discipleship practice. It is not a claim that the Bible prescribes a seven-step formula for casting out demons, and it is not a machine to be operated. It is a way of remembering the shape of a life that is being set free.

A word on sources and translations

Scripture quotations are from the Authorized (King James) Version unless otherwise noted. Where the original languages clarify a claim, the Greek or Hebrew is given. The theological and lexical foundations of this book have been checked against the companion volume, Divine Authority: Christ, Demons and Unclean Spirits, and its verification report.

The terms used for the spiritual realm are not interchangeable labels. Hebrew Satan (שָׂטָן), Greek Diabolos (διάβολος), Daimonion (δαιμόνιον), Pneuma akatharton (πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον), and Ponēros (πονηρός) have distinct uses. Care with words is part of care with people.

Safeguarding and scope

This book is a theological and pastoral resource. It is not a substitute for medical care, psychiatric care, counselling, or emergency services.

The following are non-negotiable:

  • Do not self-diagnose demon possession from dreams, intrusive thoughts, mental illness, physical symptoms, or unusual experiences.
  • Do not stop medication or abandon medical or mental-health care. Prayer and qualified care coexist; they are not competitors.
  • Do not stage unsafe confrontations, and do not substitute deliverance rituals for professional help.
  • Do not chase supernatural sensations or repeat rituals until you “feel free.” The goal is truth and obedience in Christ.
  • Seek help. Where there is psychosis, self-harm, suicide risk, abuse, addiction withdrawal, or a medical condition, contact qualified help first. A mature pastor or counsellor may walk with you, but they do not replace a clinician.

These safeguards are not a lack of faith. They are part of loving God and loving your neighbour.
